Building a Labelled Speech Corpus from Online Videos 
Goal 
Students will build a Python application that: 
1. Downloads a video from the web by pasting its link into the app. 
2. Extracts the audio track from the video. 
3. Generate a UI with spectrogram to help visualize the audio segments in the frequency 
domain. Then, segments the audio into speech, music, silence and mixed regions and 
labels each one automatically. The labeling should appear on the spectrogram with the 
ability to adjust the border between any two regions by hand. 
4. Cuts the speech and mixed regions into segments of 5 to 15 seconds at real pauses. 
5. Calculates the SNR of every segment (clean or noisy). 
6. Transcribes every speech segment with two different ASR systems and accepts the 
transcript when they agree. When they differ slightly, the annotator listens and chooses 
the correct one; otherwise, the segment is labelled difficult. 
7. Processes 3 different videos, each at least one hour long. 
8. A human annotator should be able to override any automatic decision. 
The result is a labelled speech corpus: every segment is labelled speech, music or mixed, and 
every speech segment has its SNR and its text transcription. 
Teams: 3 students. Duration: 5 weeks. Language: Python. Audio: Arabic videos (different 
Arabic countries dialect). 
Main Steps 
Step 1 — Download the Video 
• The user pastes a video link into the app and presses Download. 
• Save the video and its information: link, title, channel, duration and license. 
• Prefer Creative Commons or public-domain videos. 
• You may try: yt-dlp: https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp, or any alternative. 
Step 2 — Extract the Audio 
• Extract the audio and convert it to 16 kHz, mono, 16-bit WAV. 

• Tool: ffmpeg: https://ffmpeg.org (call it from Python with subprocess). 
Step 3 — Segment and Label Speech, Music and Mixed 
• Generate a UI with spectrogram to help visualize the audio segments in the frequency 
domain. 
• Split the whole audio into regions and label each one speech, music, silence or mixed 
(speech over music).  
• Give each region a confidence score (0 to 1) for the confidence of the automatic 
decision. 
• Use any of these tools, or another one if it works better on your videos: 
o inaSpeechSegmenter: https://github.com/ina-foss/inaSpeechSegmenter.  
o PANNs (AudioSet tags "Speech" and "Music"): 
https://github.com/qiuqiangkong/panns_inference.  
Step 4 — Cut Speech and Mixed Regions into 5–15 s Segments 
• Cut only at real pauses between words or sentences, never in the middle of a word. 
• Keep about 0.1–0.2 s of silence at each edge of a segment. 
• Tool: Silero VAD: https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad (or your own energy-based 
pause detector). 
Step 5 — Calculate the SNR 
• Compute the SNR of every segment with two methods, for example: 
o energy method: speech frames versus the quietest (pause) frames; 
o WADA-SNR (Kim and Stern, 2008). 
• Label each segment clean or noisy (start with a threshold of 20 dB and justify your final 
choice). 
Step 6 — Transcribe with Two ASR Systems 
Pass every speech segment, clean or noisy, through two different ASR systems like: 
• Whisper large-v3 (via faster-whisper): https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper  
• Meta MMS: https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all  (or another Arabic model 
from the Open Universal Arabic ASR Leaderboard on Hugging Face).  
• If one or both of the above speech recognition engines is/are not good enough, try 
other available engines.   
Before comparing, normalize the Arabic text:  
1. remove diacritics and (tatweel  (كشيدة 
2. Remove Hamzat (إ, أ, ا )to just (ا) 

3. Convert all ( ه, ة )to just (ه) 
4. Convert all (ي, ى )to (ي) 
and remove punctuation. Then compute WER between the two transcripts with jiwer 
 https://github.com/jitsi/jiwer: tool

Step 7 — Process Three Long Videos 
• Run all steps on 3 different videos, each at least one hour long (e.g. a lecture, a talk 
show with music, a documentary). 
• For every segment save: start, end, label (speech / music / mixed), SNR, clean / noisy, 
both transcripts, final transcript and status, in a CSV file per video.

Step 8 — Human Override in the App 
The annotator must be able to: 
• listen to any segment, and around any cut point; 
• change the label of any region or segment (speech / music / mixed); 

• move, split, merge, delete or add segments; 
• choose or edit the transcript when the two ASR systems differ; 
• change clean / noisy when the SNR estimate is clearly wrong; 
• save the work and continue later. 
Every human change is recorded (what was changed and by whom). Any Python UI is 
accepted, e.g. Gradio, Streamlit or PyQt.

      Deliverables 
1. Report of about 10–12 pages, IEEE style, containing: 
o the three videos (link, duration, content, license); 
o the final labeled segments of audio with the name of each segment .wav with its 
corresponding text in a .txt files.  
o the method and the parameters of each step, and why you chose them; 
o the results per video (summary table as in Step 7, segment-length histogram, SNR 
histogram); 
o a check by hand on 10 minutes per video: how many region labels were wrong, 
how many cuts fell inside a word, how many accepted transcripts had errors; 
o how many automatic decisions the annotator changed, and the review time per 
hour of audio; 
o at least 5 examples of errors, with their time in the video and the reason. 
2. Python code (download, audio, segmentation, SNR, ASR, agreement, app) with a 
README that explains how to install and run it. 
3. Results: one CSV file per video with all segments, and the segment audio files. 
4. Live demo (15 minutes): paste a new link, run the pipeline, and correct a few segments 
in the app.
